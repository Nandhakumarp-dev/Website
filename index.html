About

I am an experimental fluid mechanics researcher with a focus on spray dynamics and turbulence–particle interactions. I use optical laser diagnostics, including Particle Image Velocimetry (PIV), Interferometric Laser Imaging for Droplet Sizing (ILIDS) and high-speed imaging, to study combustion and multiphase flows.

I completed my Ph.D. in Mechanical Engineering at IIT Madras, where I was supervised by Dr. Srikrishna Sahu, and I am based in Chennai, India.

Projects

  • 2023 – 2026DST funded

    Rotary atomizer-based humidification–dehumidification desalination

    IIT Madras · Project Scientist II

    This project develops a desalination system in which a rotary atomizer breaks saline water into fine droplets inside a humidification chamber. The small droplets give a very large surface area for evaporation, so air picks up water vapor quickly; the humid air is then cooled in a dehumidifier where pure water condenses.

    My role covered the design and development of the experimental setup, and characterizing how atomizer operating conditions influence the droplets and the evaporation that drives the humidification stage.

  • Mar – Jun 2025Sakura Science LOTUS Fellowship

    Particle clustering in cloud combustion

    Hiroshima University, Japan · Visiting Researcher

    Particles in dust clouds do not distribute uniformly: turbulence gathers them into clusters separated by empty regions. This project asks how such clustering affects flame propagation and explosion behavior in aluminum dust clouds.

    I designed an experimental arrangement to generate and image particle clouds, and applied the cluster-analysis methods I developed for sprays. The work led to a paper in the Proceedings of the Combustion Institute (2026).

  • 2018 – 2024Ph.D. research

    Droplet clustering and group vaporization in turbulent evaporating sprays

    IIT Madras · Supervisor: Dr. Srikrishna Sahu

    Sprays are usually modeled as isolated droplets, but real droplets are bunched into clusters and voids. My doctoral research measured how this clustering develops in space, how turbulence affects it, and how droplets in a cluster evaporate collectively, so that a group vaporizes differently from the same droplets in isolation.

    The work used laser-based diagnostics, including PIV and ILIDS, with MATLAB image processing to extract droplet size, position and velocity. It produced publications in the Journal of Fluid Mechanics, Physics of Fluids and the Proceedings of the Combustion Institute.

  • 2015 – 2017M.E. thesis

    Direct contact membrane distillation

    College of Engineering Guindy · Supervisors: Dr. R. Velraj and Dr. Raju Abraham

    Membrane distillation is a thermally driven desalination process in which water vapor passes through a hydrophobic membrane from a hot feed to a cool permeate stream. My thesis was an experimental analysis of the direct contact configuration, studying how operating conditions affect the permeate output.

Selected publications

  • 2026Physics of Fluids 38(9), 093324

    Droplet clustering and collective gasification in reacting and non-reacting swirling fuel sprays

    M. Balasubramaniyan, N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    Swirling co-flow air is widely used to stabilize flames and improve fuel–air mixing, but its effect on how droplets group together is not well understood. This paper examines how swirl influences droplet clustering and the evaporation rate in reacting and non-reacting sprays.

    Comparing the two conditions shows how the flame changes cluster formation and collective gasification relative to the cold spray.

  • 2026Proc. Combust. Inst. 42, 106646

    Particle clustering and its role in aluminum dust cloud explosions

    N. Pandurangan, R. Inoue, A. Ueda, S. Sahu, W. Kim

    Aluminum dust explosions are a serious industrial hazard, and the spatial arrangement of particles in the cloud is thought to matter as much as the average concentration. This study investigates how particles cluster in aluminum dust clouds.

    Voronoi analysis is used to quantify the clustering and to predict the probability of explosion, linking cloud structure to explosion risk.

  • 2025Physics of Fluids 37(3)

    Dynamics of mustard seed spluttering and popping in heated cooking oil

    K. Kumar, A. Singh, N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    When mustard seeds are added to hot oil they pop and spatter droplets, a familiar kitchen phenomenon with real fluid-mechanical content. This paper studies the size of the oil droplets ejected during the process.

    It also presents an analytical model for the popping mechanism that explains how the seed bursts.

  • 2024J. Fluid Mech. 998, A26

    Analysis of group evaporation characteristics of droplet clusters in an evaporating spray

    N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    Droplets inside a cluster share a vapor-rich surrounding, which slows their evaporation compared with an isolated droplet. This paper analyzes the group evaporation of droplets in an acetone spray.

    The analysis is used to predict the evaporation rate of droplet groups, a step towards spray models that account for clustering.

  • 2024Proc. Combust. Inst. 40(1–4), 105434

    Group evaporation of small- and large-scale droplet clusters in a fuel spray-laden homogeneous and isotropic turbulent airflow

    N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    Clusters exist at many length scales in a spray. This paper examines how strongly turbulence in a homogeneous and isotropic airflow affects the evaporation of small- and large-scale droplet clusters.

    It shows that cluster size matters for how droplet groups respond to the surrounding turbulence.

  • 2023Proc. Combust. Inst. 39(2), 2653–2662

    Droplet cluster evolution and collective gasification of droplet groups in a fuel spray: A comparative study under non-reacting and reacting conditions

    M. Balasubramaniyan, N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    This study follows the formation and evolution of droplet clusters in a fuel spray and the group behavior of the droplets within them.

    Measurements under non-reacting and reacting conditions are compared to show how combustion alters clustering and collective gasification.

  • 2022Physics of Fluids 34(11)

    Spatial evolution of multi-scale droplet clusters in an evaporating spray

    N. Pandurangan, S. Sahu

    This paper examines how droplet clusters form and evolve in space across multiple scales in an evaporating spray, and quantifies the degree of clustering.

    It shows that evaporation itself enhances droplet clustering, so clustering cannot be treated as a purely aerodynamic effect.
